# IO (input/output) board specification

What the IO board has to do, with the numbers the tree fixes. The IO
board is the coprocessor board. It carries everything with a deadline:
outputs, receiver inputs, sensor front ends, the power path and the CAN
(Controller Area Network) link to the display. The display is the ESP32-S3
panel in `firmware/panel/`.

**Status: draft.** No schematic exists. Round 1 of
[the research](Research.md) ran from 2026-09-28 to 2026-09-30 and names the
part on each line it selected. [Parts](Parts.md) lists every part with its
stock, and the page of each research group gives the choice, the
alternatives and the reason: [Control](Control.md) for R1 to R4,
[Supply](Supply.md) for R5 to R8 and [Sensing](Sensing.md) for R9 to R13. A
line with no part says so and is stated there as not known or not verified.
A line marked _open_ waits on a question in [Research](Research.md).

Sources are files and lines in the tree, pull request #167, the pico-sdk 2.3.1
headers, two pico-sdk 2.3.0 sources (`boards/olimex_rp2350_xl.h` and
`src/rp2_common/hardware_pio/pio.c`), and two datasheets read from copies
outside the tree: the RP2350 datasheet (build 2024-08-08) and the W25Q16JV
datasheet (revision D, 2016-08-12). R1 reads the RP2350 datasheet from
Raspberry Pi. Build 2025-07-29 of it, the copy Raspberry Pi serves, numbers

1424 (GPIO pins; GPIO is general-purpose input/output) is 1427, 1425 (QSPI

times this page cites from revision D, and gives the endurance without "per

## Microcontroller, flash and debug

The RP2354B runs every function on the IO board that has a deadline. Its 2 MB
of in-package flash holds the firmware image. Q8 moves the output binding to
an I²C FRAM, MB85RC256VPNF-G-AMERE2 (owner, 2026-09-29). The core executes from that flash.
Every erase and every page program therefore holds interrupts off, and the core
answers nothing while it runs. Firmware loads over the RP2354B's own USB, and
the console is on USB only. Two dry contacts, BOOT and RESET, let the
measurement bench restart the board and put it in its USB loader with nobody
at it. If the bench reflashes the coprocessor between runs, it does so over
SWD.

Three places in the coprocessor build assume more than 2 MB of flash: the
board file, the output store's offset and the post-link image-size check. All
three change before the first IO board build
([Research](Research.md#consequence-of-the-rp2354bs-2-mb-flash)). The board
number, the reserved pins and the pin catalogue are compiled into the image,
so the IO board's build carries its own copy of
`firmware/iomcu/include/iomcu_pins.h` and a new row in `k_boards`. The
converter for the accelerometer (Q4): see [Measurement](#measurement).

## Link and safety

The IO board is one of the two nodes on a classic CAN bus to the display.
Every command and every reading crosses it, and the coprocessor transmits only
in answer to a request. On the bring-up module the CAN controller sits on SPI
behind a 3.3 V transceiver. R2 chooses the IO board's controller and
transceiver.

The display's safety line carries edges, one every 20 ms, not a level. Two
watchers sit on it. Firmware on the coprocessor rejects intervals outside 4 to
150 ms and runs on the bring-up module. A retriggerable monostable removes the
output drive and opens the servo and ESC power switches when the edges stop,
with no firmware in the path. Pull request #167 specifies the monostable. It
is on no board. R3 ([Research](Research.md#research-categories)) chooses its
parts, and R4 the output buffer, to the text of pull request #167 at commit
23c82ca, not merged. Prerequisite 4 ([Research](Research.md#prerequisites))
fetches that commit. No hardware in this design removes the outputs when the
coprocessor stops acting and the display keeps beating.

## Outputs, programming, receiver inputs and ESC telemetry

This part carries every signal between the RP2354B and a servo, an ESC, a
programmer or a receiver. It has 20 typed output ports (owner, 2026-09-25):
16 PWM ports and 4 multiprotocol ports. A PWM port is enabled or disabled and
carries a pulse range; its frame rate is set per pair of ports, because the two
channels of one PWM slice share a period, and one PWM port can switch to PPM. A
multiprotocol port is set to servo PWM, DShot, bidirectional DShot, ESC
telemetry, servo configuration or ESC bootloader, so the programmer's protocols
run on these 4 ports and no separate programming connector exists. Bidirectional
DShot on all 4 takes 8 of the 12 PIO state machines and 64 of the 96 instruction words. The board also has
receiver inputs of one signal wire per bus, a one-pin UART socket for OpenYGE ESC
telemetry and other serial devices, and an external CAN port on a
controller of its own. The 20 ports and the UART socket leave the board through
the output gate of the heartbeat monostable ([Link and safety](#link-and-safety)),
and the external CAN transceiver is in standby while the enable is low.

Some lines carry both directions on one wire. Bidirectional DShot releases the
line after each frame and reads the ESC's reply on the same pin. The one-wire
bootloader and OpenYGE are half duplex. A one-way buffer on such a line blocks
the receive direction. PIO reach and the PWM slices limit the pin map: see
[Microcontroller, flash and debug](#microcontroller-flash-and-debug).

## Power

The IO board runs from a 12 to 20 V DC input when one is present. Otherwise it
runs from the bench's own 2S pack. The selection is automatic. The selected
source runs the IO board and, through the link cable, the display. The pack
is an SLS XTRON 3000 mAh 2S1P pack (owner, 2026-09-30). A hardware disconnect removes the pack when
any cell falls to 3.0 V, read per cell from the pack's balance lead (F9).
From the selected source the board makes the 3.3 V logic rail, a 5 V rail,
3 servo rails and the adjustable supply's output. It charges the pack from USB-C only, drawing up to 3 A at 5 V, and never from
the DC input (F8).

Three servo rails, each from its own TPS55285 buck-boost converter, feed the
20 output ports: one rail feeds 8 PWM ports, one the other 8 PWM ports, and one
the 4 multiprotocol ports (owner, 2026-09-30). Each rail's voltage is set on
its own, so LV (low-voltage) and HV (high-voltage) servos run at the same time,
and an ESC on the multiprotocol ports runs independent of the servos. Each port has its own supply switch and its own current monitor channel; the owner's choice is the INA3221, a seed that R8 may replace (owner, 2026-09-27).
The servo procedures that read the port monitors are written and host-tested
against a modelled servo. The coprocessor image does not call them
(`firmware/iomcu/CMakeLists.txt:38-41`). Round 1 of the research re-checks the
TPS55285 and selects the integrated circuits of R3 and R5 to R8, the
converter's inductor, the motor shunts and the switch of
the ESC pack. R5 selects the power input's protection. Round 2 selects the
other passives, the connectors, and the protection on the signal, sensor,
balance-lead, link and heartbeat connectors. Round 3 covers the thermal design
and the busbar, mounting and cabling of the external 300 A path
([Research](Research.md#scope)).

The switches on the servo rails and the ESC pack, their timing and the
interlock supply: see [Link and safety](#link-and-safety).

## Measurement

The IO board measures the motor current, the pack voltage, the cells on the
balance lead, rotation, vibration, temperatures, thrust and torque. It also
reads sensors on external I²C ports. The coprocessor publishes voltage,
current, power, rpm, the ESC's and the motor's temperatures, charge and energy
on the BENCH page (0x20). Voltage, current, rpm and each temperature carry a
valid bit. No link page carries the cells, vibration, thrust or torque: the per-cell voltages the cell monitor of R9 reads, and the BATTERY screen's resistances, capacity and drawn charge, have no register, and only the host tests and the screen renderer call `battery_screen_set()` (`test/host/test_battery.c:59`, `test/host/render_screen.c:324`). A link page for them is a firmware change outside round 1; until it lands the cell monitor's line is complete in hardware and open on the link. The
display draws a quantity that nothing measures as an empty field. The bring-up
module has no measurement front end. There the coprocessor reports only what an
ESC sends: speed from bidirectional DShot replies, and voltage, current and
temperature from extended DShot telemetry. Neither path has run against an ESC.
No quantity in this section has been measured on hardware.

The onboard shunt carries up to 150 A on the same board as the 3.3 V I²C bus.
A current above 150 A, to 300 A and more, flows through an external shunt, and
only its sense leads reach the board. The two paths replace each other: a test
is cabled through one of them, never both (owner, 2026-09-25). The onboard shunt's 150 A is continuous (F2). The ESC
pack has up to 16 cells, 67.2 V at 4.2 V a cell (F15). The
accelerometer and the index pulse are sampled on one timebase. The vibration
path resolves the 167 Hz fundamental at 10,000 rpm. The capability bits and the
pin catalogue's sensor holder: see
[Microcontroller, flash and debug](#microcontroller-flash-and-debug).

### Not known

- The part for every connector, including the connector the gated outputs leave by, which pull request #167 leaves unspecified. Round 2 of [the research](Research.md) selects connectors; round 3 covers mechanical design and layout constraints.
- Where the test points sit, and whether each carries a series resistor of its own. No research category or question covers test access. The bench puts a series resistor in each analyser lead, given as "a few hundred ohms" with no value (`testbench/WIRING.md:23`).
- How one device's supply is removed alone for test steps 3, 4 and 13: whether the board provides a way to open it or the lead is lifted by hand. Pull request #167 says "supply lead lifted" and no more. No question covers it.
- Whether the connector layout fits two rows on one pitch. A layout that does not fit is listed and not drawn (`docs/Link.md:134`). Settled by the layout, round 3.
- How the pads page states the 3 servo rails, each with two settings, up to 5.5 V and up to 8.4 V. Not stated in the tree. A rail above 25.5 V has no value on the pads page (`shared/link/include/link_pages.h:476-479`). The ESC pack reaches 67.2 V (F15), so the pads page cannot state it.
- The connectors for the encoder (single-ended and RS-422 on one input, F4), the 3 load-cell channels (F3) and the infrared and thermocouple motor temperature channels (F5): not chosen. Round 2 selects them. The 2 external I²C ports take Qwiic connectors, JST SH with 4 pins (Q7); round 2 selects the part.
- Where the encoder's two jumpers (signal form and supply) and the 20 port ceiling jumpers (F16) sit: not stated. Round 3 covers the layout.
- Whether RXCAN and TXCAN, between the MCP2518FDT-E/SL controller and the TCAN3413DR transceiver (R2), are reachable for test on the IO board: not stated.
- How the bench's stimulus and responder board reaches the IO board's output, programming and receiver connectors. `testbench/README.md:331-339` gives the roles; `testbench/WIRING.md:287-289` names a stimulus generator the guide does not describe.

## Conflicts in the tree

Places where two sources in the tree, or the tree and pull request #167, state
different things. Each entry names both sources. Where this page follows one
of them, the entry says which and why.

- **Monostable window.** `docs/Safety.md:32-37`, `STATUS.md:40-41`, `STATUS.md:325`, `testbench/WIRING.md:497` and `testbench/WIRING.md:511-512` give about 150 ms. In pull request #167 the enable node falls 155 to 185 ms after the last edge. It is set to 170 ms on test, and the deadline is 200 ms. There 150 ms is `HEARTBEAT_MAX_GAP_MS`, the longest gap the firmware accepts. A window at 150 ms would drop the enable on a gap the firmware accepts. This page follows pull request #167, the specification R3 builds to.
- **The servo rail current.** Pull request #167 at 23c82ca gives the servo rail as up to 8.4 V and 4 to 8 A, from the `hardware/docs/Power.md` of that commit, which gives the TPS55288. The owner decision (Servo supply and Servo rail in [hardware STATUS](../STATUS.md#decided); [Power](Power.md#servo-supply-tps55285)) gives the TPS55285, whose limit stops at 6.35 A. This page follows the owner decision: 4 to 6.35 A a rail, on 3 rails (owner, 2026-09-30).
- **The latch and the clear network.** Pull request #167 specifies a monostable that does not latch: the enable returns on the first resumed edge, and its test steps 7 and 10 assume that. It clears the monostable through a resistor-capacitor network, qualified for a fast power-up ramp only; it states no ramp time. The owner's answers add a hardware latch with an operator clear control (F11) and put a reset supervisor in place of the network (F12). This page follows F11 and F12; the test steps that assume no latch change with the parts R3 selects.
- **Where the monostable is specified.** `STATUS.md:308` names `docs/Safety.md` and `testbench/WIRING.md` as the specification. R3 ([Research](Research.md#research-categories)) builds to pull request #167 at commit 23c82ca. This page follows pull request #167.
- **Interlock test pass figure.** `testbench/WIRING.md:705` passes the interlock test when the enable is deasserted within 150 ms of the last edge. Under pull request #167 the enable falls no sooner than 155 ms, so a correct build fails that check; test step 7 of pull request #167 passes 155 to 185 ms.
- **Switched rail deadline.** `testbench/WIRING.md:557-560` treats the switched rail being down within 150 ms as the deadline. Pull request #167 leaves the rail's decay out of the timing budget, measured and recorded, not specified; its ESC example with 470 µF and a 50 mA idle draw needs up to 420 ms after the last edge (test step 9).
- **Order of the two watchers.** `docs/Safety.md:49` lets the monostable act "sooner or later by its own timing" than the firmware. Pull request #167 fixes the order: the firmware disarms at 150 ms, then the enable node falls between 155 and 185 ms.
- **Pin tolerance.** `testbench/README.md:410` says the RP2350's pins are not 5 V tolerant. `STATUS.md:317-319` admits a 5 V transceiver output on bank 0 through a 2.2 kΩ series resistor. Pull request #167 says the RP2350 is not specified as tolerating an input above an absent IOVDD. The RP2350 datasheet (build 2024-08-08, Tables 1423, 1424 and 1430, read from a copy) rates GPIO0 to 39 at 5.5 V while IOVDD is at 3.3 V and at 3.63 V while IOVDD is at 0 V, and GPIO40 to 47 at IOVDD + 0.5 V. This page follows the datasheet. P1 checks the statement in pull request #167 against those tables. R1 confirms the ratings in T2; where R1's figures differ, P5 checks the statement again.
- **Where the monostable sits, and the heartbeat's path.** `firmware/iomcu/include/iomcu_pins.h:36-38` and `docs/FirstRun.md:113` route the heartbeat to GP3 through the monostable. `firmware/iomcu/src/main.c:451` and `firmware/panel/main/main.c:251` place the monostable on a daughterboard. Pull request #167 puts GP3 on the node beside the trigger input, as a listener, and leaves the board unspecified; R3 ([Research](Research.md#research-categories)) places the monostable in the IO board's research. This page follows pull request #167 for the node and lists the board as not known.
- **Heartbeat source.** `firmware/iomcu/src/main.c:456` describes the source as a 39 Hz render loop, and `firmware/panel/main/main.c:249-250` as one GPIO write per frame. `shared/safety/include/heartbeat.h:37-45` and `docs/Safety.md:32-34` put the generator in the 5 ms control task with an edge every 20 ms. This page follows `heartbeat.h`, because the generator runs on `HEARTBEAT_PERIOD_MS` from the control task, which `STATUS.md:37-39` also states.
- **The display's power.** Pull request #167 states that the panel is powered on its own USB and beats whether or not the coprocessor board has power, and `testbench/WIRING.md:74-75` powers each board from its own USB. The owner decision of 2026-09-24 (owner decisions in [Research](Research.md#scope); Board power in [hardware STATUS](../STATUS.md#decided)) powers the display through the link cable. The powered-off isolation rule of pull request #167 applies either way.
- **Where the servo rail comes from.** `testbench/WIRING.md:77` feeds the servo and ESC rails from their own supplies, never from a board's rail. Servo supply and Servo rail in [hardware STATUS](../STATUS.md#decided) make the servo rail on the IO board with the TPS55285. This page follows the owner decision: the IO board makes the servo rail. `testbench/WIRING.md:77` describes the bring-up bench.
- **The pack voltage.** Pull request #167 (test step 9) says the ESC path's pack voltage is not chosen. The owner's answer to F15 ([Research](Research.md#blocking-answered-before-the-research-tasks)) is 16 cells, 67.2 V at 4.2 V a cell (owner, 2026-09-25), 17.8 V under the INA238's 85 V bus rating ([Power](Power.md#motor-monitor-ina228)). [Power](Power.md#summary) sizes the motor monitor for 67.2 V. This page follows F15.
- **The current through the board.** [Power](Power.md#summary), [Power](Power.md#motor-monitor-ina228) and pull request #167 (Not specified) put a 300 A path on the pack and beside the I²C bus. The owner decision (Motor current path in [hardware STATUS](../STATUS.md#decided)) puts up to 150 A through the board and 300 A and above through an external shunt reached by its sense leads. This page follows the owner decision.
- **The per-socket voltage ceiling.** `shared/ui/stub_screen.c:56` lists "Socket ceilings: low and high voltage, each set in hardware". The question F16 ([Research](Research.md#blocking-answered-before-the-research-tasks)) states that no source in the tree gives it. The owner's answer to F16 matches the stub: a jumper on each of the 20 ports selects an LV or an HV ceiling (owner, 2026-09-25). This page follows F16.
- **The servo rail monitor.** `docs/Manifest.md:28` records one monitor on the servo rail. The owner decision (Output ports in [hardware STATUS](../STATUS.md#decided)) puts a current monitor channel on each of the 20 output ports (owner, 2026-09-25). The owner's choice for it is the INA3221, a seed that R8 may replace (Port monitors in [hardware STATUS](../STATUS.md#decided); [Power](Power.md#summary); owner, 2026-09-27). This page follows the owner decision: one monitor per port.
- **The SIMULATED bit.** `docs/Link.md:156-157` and `shared/link/include/link_pages.h:270-275` say a coprocessor without a measurement front end sets BENCH flag bit 7 and the panel draws SIMULATION. `firmware/iomcu/src/main.c:649` and `STATUS.md:62-64` say the bit is never set by a coprocessor that answers. The Valid bits row in [Measurement](#measurement) follows the firmware, which is what runs.
- **What a clear capability bit does.** `shared/link/include/link_pages.h:170-172` says a screen whose capability is missing opens and runs from the model, marked MODELLED. `firmware/panel/main/main.c:2298-2314` (`read_bench()`) uses the coprocessor's BENCH page whenever the coprocessor answers, whatever the capability word holds. `STATUS.md:62-65` says a coprocessor that answers reports only what it measures, the panel models only when nothing answers, and the two never appear on one screen. The word reaches the menu and the overview only: `shared/ui/overview_screen.c:165-177` puts a MODELLED badge on a tile whose bits are clear. The Capability word row in [Microcontroller, flash and debug](#microcontroller-flash-and-debug) follows `main.c` and `STATUS.md`, which is what runs.
- **Samples a poll sees.** `shared/link/include/link_pages.h:226-227` and `firmware/iomcu/src/main.c:678-679` say the panel sees one poll in fifty of the coprocessor's samples. `firmware/iomcu/src/main.c:1011-1016` samples every 20 ms and the panel polls every 50 ms (`docs/Link.md:167`), 2.5 samples a poll.
- **The DShot frame rate against the poll.** `docs/DShot.md:165` and `firmware/iomcu/src/outputs_hw.c:23-24` say 1 kHz is twenty times the panel's poll rate. The panel polls every 50 ms (`docs/Link.md:167`), 20 Hz, so 1 kHz is fifty times the poll rate. Twenty times is the ratio to the coprocessor's 50 Hz sampling (`firmware/iomcu/src/main.c:1011-1016`), which `firmware/iomcu/src/outputs_hw.c:26` names.
- **The first fetch of the board picture.** `shared/link/include/link_pages.h:393-396` and `docs/Screens.md:519-521` give about ten seconds. `docs/FirstRun.md:137` gives about a minute for the first link-up, and `docs/FirstRun.md:154-155` tens of seconds of extra traffic. Whether the minute of the first link-up covers traffic other than the picture is not stated.
- **Receiver framing.** `STATUS.md:13-16` and `STATUS.md:23` put receiver framing and the receiver buses on the coprocessor. `docs/Building.md:63` and `firmware/iomcu/CMakeLists.txt:32-43` build the S.BUS decoder for the panel and the host suite, not the coprocessor.
- **A released PWM pin.** `firmware/iomcu/src/out_pwm.h:43` says a released pin is left low. `firmware/iomcu/src/out_pwm.c:172-175` returns it to a plain input with no drive, so the line's bias sets its level. This page follows the code, which is what runs.

The module build's header, `pimoroni_pico_plus2_rp2350`, sets `PICO_RP2350A` to 0, so `NUM_BANK0_GPIOS` is 48 in the module build (pico-sdk 2.3.1). On the module a pin from GP30 to GP47 is then bound, drives nothing and reads back as working, the case `firmware/iomcu/include/iomcu_pins.h:94-96` describes. This page follows the pico-sdk headers.
